Notes on the parts of the job that don't show up in a screenshot — competitive game architecture, live-service systems, and two years spent building games you play with real objects.

  • When there's money on the result, balance stops being a feeling

    Brick Blaster Cash · competitive mobile · ~6 min

    Every developer tunes by feel. That loop works right up until a match has real money on the outcome — then the score distribution becomes the product, and your own play sessions become the worst possible evidence.

  • Never let the client decide the rules

    Addressables & server-authoritative config · architecture · ~7 min

    How fast can we ship a change, and can we trust what the player's phone tells us? Two problems that look unrelated, one architectural decision that answers both — and what it costs.

  • Games you play with real objects

    Alienide Interactive, 2017–2019 · XR & physical interfaces · ~7 min

    A surfboard, a bicycle, and a cricket bat with a controller in the handle. There's no Stack Overflow answer for a plywood rig — and standing next to strangers while they play teaches you things no dashboard will.
